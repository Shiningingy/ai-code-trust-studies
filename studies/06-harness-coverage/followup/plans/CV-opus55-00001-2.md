## What a near-miss means here

The query needs all four conditions to hold: c1 coastal employer, c2 three or more distinct events this year, c3 avoids dairy, c4 routinely uses public transport. A near-miss is a person the pipeline rejects, but who might qualify if a single judgment went the other way.

**Definition.** A person is a near-miss if three conditions are positively established and the fourth falls into one of these cases:

- **Unresolved:** no evidence was found at all, for example no employment sentence, or an organization with no known city.
- **Borderline count:** for c2, exactly 2 distinct this-year events.
- **Weak negative:** the fourth condition failed only because of a judgment call. Examples are a mention I labeled ambiguous (`B. Novak` fitting two roster people), a phrasing I classed as negative or past ("dislikes eating milk"), or a conflicting city record.

A person with a clear, correctly attributed negative ("Quinn Reed routinely travels by bus but no longer does") is not a near-miss. I'd still list them so I can check the attribution.

**How I'd find them.** The script builds a 157 × 4 matrix in which each cell is TRUE, FALSE-explicit, FALSE-weak or UNKNOWN. Each cell keeps the sentence IDs that support it. Near-misses are the rows with three TRUE cells and one weak or UNKNOWN cell. For each one I'd run Grep over every name form (case-insensitive, initials, aliases), then Read the passages it hits. I'd do the same for all full matches, to check precision.

## Why that isn't enough

The near-miss check uses the same patterns as the main pass. If a person's job is phrased "is employed by" and their transport as "takes the metro daily", they show up with only two TRUE cells and are never reviewed. Lowering the threshold to two TRUE cells doesn't fix this, because the failure scales with how many conditions the patterns miss. Recall has to stop depending on the patterns.

## Revised plan

The change is to account for every sentence, not just the ones that match.

1. **Parse.** Same as before: split into passages and sentences, handling initials.
2. **Normalize sentences into templates** with a Bash/Python script:
   - Replace roster names and aliases with `<NAME>`, `P-\d+` with `<PID>`, `Org(anization) \d+` with `<ORG>`, event IDs with `<EVT>`, and known cities with `<CITY>`.
   - Replace the remaining capitalized name-like tokens with `<UNK>`, which catches names missing from the roster.
   - Count the distinct templates with `sort | uniq -c`.

   With roughly 2,400 passages, I'd expect tens to low hundreds of templates rather than thousands. That makes it practical to read all of them.
3. **Label every template.** Each one gets a label: roster, employment-current, employment-past, org-location, volunteer-this-year, volunteer-last-year, dairy-current, dairy-distractor, transport-habitual, transport-past/one-off/future, or filler. Anything unlabeled is **residue**. I'd Read the residue in batches and either label it or add a pattern, repeating until no residue is left. That leaves no fact-bearing phrasing unexamined, even when no person shows a pattern of near-misses.
4. **Attach unnamed subjects.** Sentences starting "She/He/They", and quoted "I" statements, are attached to the nearest named person in the same passage and flagged as inferred. Any `<UNK>` name is checked against the roster for spelling and case variants. Names that still don't match go on an unresolved-identity list.
5. **Build per-person dossiers.** For each ID, collect every sentence that mentions any of their name forms, including the inferred ones. Any person whose dossier has a sentence that matched no condition pattern but contains a domain keyword (diet, food, travel, commute, work, job, employer, event, volunteer, help) gets a manual Read. This review is triggered by unexplained evidence, not by how many conditions passed.
6. **Build the matrix and review.** Fill in the matrix. Then Read the dossiers of all full matches, all near-misses as defined above, and all rows with any UNKNOWN cell plus at least two TRUE cells.
7. **Run a consistency check.** Each person should have at most one current employer, and each organization one city. Every this-year event should be credited to the volunteer and not to the P-ID inside the event name. Conflicts are resolved by Read.
8. **Output** the sorted IDs.

## What the revision costs

- **Scripted parts** (templating, dossiers, the matrix with evidence IDs): about 3 to 5 more Bash runs. This is cheap, since the file is only 469 KB.
- **Reading:** the main cost. I'd review the template list and residue (perhaps 100 to 300 lines) and dossiers for perhaps 20 to 50 people instead of about 10 to 20. That's roughly 30,000 to 60,000 extra tokens of reading, and about 15 to 25 more Grep, Read and Bash calls.
- **Overall:** about 1.5 to 2 times the original plan. Reading the whole file would cost about 120,000 tokens or more, so this stays well below that, while still covering every sentence through templates instead of skimming.
