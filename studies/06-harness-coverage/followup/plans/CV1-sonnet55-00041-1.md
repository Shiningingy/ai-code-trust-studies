## What a near-miss means

A **near-miss** is a person who satisfies all but a small number of the query's conditions. Say the query reduces to N atomic conditions, C1…CN, from step 2. Then:

- **Qualifying:** all N conditions are confirmed by the text.
- **Near-miss (k=1):** exactly N−1 conditions are confirmed and one is contradicted or not stated.
- **Near-miss (k=2):** exactly N−2 are confirmed. I'd only use this if N is 5 or more.

A near-miss matters for two reasons:
- It is the likeliest false positive. It's also the likeliest true positive that I've wrongly rejected, because the one "failed" condition may just be a fact my pattern missed.
- Each near-miss must be sorted into one of two groups. In the first, the failing condition is explicitly contradicted, for example a different date or a negation. That person is excluded. In the second, the condition is only unstated or phrased in a way my search missed. That person needs a targeted re-read.

**How I'd find them with my tools:**
1. Write one Grep pattern per condition, with synonyms and variants. Each pattern would run with `-n`, with `output_mode: "content"`.
2. Save the per-condition hits to a scratchpad file, keyed by person and document.
3. Count confirmed conditions per person. People at N are candidates. People at N−1 (and N−2 for large N) are near-misses.
4. For each near-miss, Read the passages around that person's mentions. Then decide whether the missing condition is contradicted or just unfound.
