**What a near-miss means here.** The query is a conjunction of five conditions, c1 to c5. A near-miss is a person who satisfies exactly four of them, or who fails one only because my evidence for it is ambiguous. I'd define it precisely as follows:

- **Strict near-miss:** exactly one condition is definitively false, and the other four are supported by evidence. These people are not answers. They are an audit set. If one of them fails on a condition for a wording-variant reason, I've found a pattern gap.
- **Soft near-miss:** a person whose failing condition has no matching evidence at all, as opposed to evidence that contradicts it. Missing evidence and contradicting evidence are different cases. Only missing evidence can hide a true match.
- **Not a near-miss:** a person who fails two or more conditions, or whose failure rests on explicit contradicting text. An example is "attended only in the past" for c2.

To find them, my script would compute a per-person vector of five states: true, false-by-evidence, and no-evidence. I'd list everyone with at least four true or soft-true. I'd read their passages by hand and check whether the missing fact exists in a phrasing my pattern didn't catch.

**Where that check fails.** A near-miss check only catches a person for whom at least four conditions were detected. If my patterns miss facts for several of a person's conditions, that person might show only two or three true conditions. They'd look like a clear non-match and never reach the audit set. The near-miss check assumes my extraction is mostly right, and that assumption is what's in doubt.

The script would use no relation-specific patterns for this step, only name matching. That makes it independent of the phrasing of each fact.

3. **Read dossiers directly.** For every person with at least one hit on any condition, I'd read their dossier with Read and judge all five conditions from the text. If the pool is too large, I'd use a loose threshold, such as at least two stems matching. I'd check the distribution of hits first so I know how many people each threshold leaves out.
4. **Run the strict patterns as a cross-check.** I'd compare the two methods. Wherever they disagree, I'd read the passages and settle it by hand. Disagreements show which phrasings the patterns miss. I'd then fix the patterns and rerun.
5. **Check coverage.** I'd count passages not assigned to any person, and passages that mention a condition keyword but produced no tuple. I'd read a sample of both sets.
