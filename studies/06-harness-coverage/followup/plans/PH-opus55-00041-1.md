I've looked through `context.md`. It has 2,327 short "Passage" records, and the query comes at the end. The query asks for the person IDs of everyone who meets all five conditions. Here is how I would answer it, and where it could go wrong.

## What the data looks like
- **Names and IDs:** 153 lines read "the community roster records *Full Name* under identifier P-### and also uses the familiar names *X. Surname*". Everywhere else, people are named by full name, by initial plus surname ("O. Chen"), or with a lowercase first name ("omar Chen").
- **Facts:** each is a fixed sentence, and each condition has look-alike sentences that don't count:
  - **c1 (three or more events this year):** "volunteered at event-P-xxx-n during this-year" counts. "volunteered at old-event-P-xxx during last-year" doesn't.
  - **c2 (guardian of a youth in an evening course):**
    - "is the legal guardian of Youth 075 / Y075" counts. "is an emergency contact, but not a guardian, for…" (about 60 lines) doesn't.
    - The youth must "currently attend evening_*". "daytime_first_aid" and past-only attendance don't count.
  - **c3 (prefers train):** "genuinely prefers train to plane" counts. "chose train … only because of a disruption" and "likes train and plane equally" don't.
  - **c4 (eats fruit):** "enjoys eating X" counts if X is a fruit (watermelon, banana, pear, mango, peach, apple, orange, plum). "dislikes eating apple" and "enjoys eating lentils/bread/rice" don't.
  - **c5 (avoids dairy):** "currently chooses to avoid dairy products" counts. "is temporarily skipping cheese/milk/butter", "dislikes eating milk" and "buys cheese for other people" don't.
- **Layout:** passages mix in filler ("Elsewhere in the note, …"), several facts per sentence joined by commas, and quoted speech ("Jia Haddad said, '…I. Bennett used to…'").

## Steps
1. **Build the name list.** Use Grep/Bash to pull every roster line into a table of P-ID, full name and familiar names. Then check that no initial plus surname points to two people. My quick check found no clashes among rostered names, but I'll confirm it against the full familiar-name lists.
2. **Normalise names.** Write a small script in the scratchpad that fixes lowercase first names and maps every full name or alias to a P-ID. Any name it can't resolve gets logged, not dropped.
3. **Split sentences into facts.** Split each passage on sentence and clause boundaries (". ", ", and", ", while", ", but", quotes). Then match the exact patterns above, and record each fact as (person, fact type, value, passage ID).
4. **Check each condition per person:**
   - **c1:** count the *distinct* this-year event IDs, so an event mentioned twice only counts once. The person needs three or more.
   - **c2:** join their legal-guardian records to a youth ID, treating "Youth 075" and "Y075" as the same. Then check that the youth has a current evening_* course.
   - **c3, c4, c5:** need the positive wording above. For c4, the food must be on a fruit list I set out explicitly.
5. **Check for conflicts.** Grep for later statements that might cancel an earlier one, such as "no longer" or a contrary preference.
6. **Find what the patterns missed.** Grep each keyword area (volunteer, guardian, train, eating, dairy, attends) for lines my patterns didn't capture, and read them with the Read tool. This catches rewordings I haven't seen yet.
7. **Verify by hand.** For each candidate who passes, and for near-misses that pass four of the five, re-read the source passages with Grep context and confirm each fact.
8. **Write the answer:** a sorted JSON list of P-IDs, `{"matches": [...]}`.

## What could make me miss someone
- An alias that doesn't resolve, which splits one person's facts across two entries. This matters most for c1, where events add up.
- A fact buried in a long comma-joined sentence or inside quoted speech.
- A youth ID written in an unfamiliar way, which breaks the c2 link.
- Wording I haven't seen yet for any of the conditions.

## What could make me include someone wrongly
- Counting the decoys listed above (emergency contacts, last-year events, "skipping cheese", train chosen only because of a disruption, liking train and plane equally, disliked fruit) as matches.
- Counting the same event twice.
- Giving a fact to the wrong person when several are packed into one sentence.
- Matching an initial to the wrong person if two people turn out to share it.
- Missing a past-only or "used to" qualifier on a youth's course.
