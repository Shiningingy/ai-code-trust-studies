## Plan

**Step 1: Build the name-to-ID map (Bash/Python script, output saved to the scratchpad).**
- Extract each `records <Full Name> under identifier P-NNN … familiar names <aliases>`.
- Match names case-insensitively. The text has lowercase forms like "omar Chen" and "f. Chen".
- Expand each person to three forms: full name, the listed aliases, and the initial form ("R. Chen"). Count how many roster people share each alias. If one is shared, mentions of it are ambiguous and can't be credited to either person without other evidence. My quick check found no initial collisions among roster people. A rostered "R. Chen" could still collide with a non-rostered "Ravi Chen", so I'll also check initials against every full name in the text.
- Inside quotes like `Jia Haddad said, "I …"`, "I" means the speaker. Other names inside the quote are third parties.

**Step 2: Extract facts clause by clause with regexes, not sentence by sentence.** Many sentences chain several people with commas ("…, while…", "and…"), so each fact must attach to the name right before its verb phrase.

- **c1 (volunteering):** count only `volunteered at event-P-NNN-N during this-year`. I also saw one "during this year" variant, so allow both spellings. Exclude the `old-event-P-NNN during last-year` decoys. Count **distinct** event IDs per person, because the same event can be mentioned more than once. The person needs 3 or more.
- **c2 (guardian of an evening student):**
  - Count `X is the legal guardian of Youth N` and the first-person `"I am the legal guardian of Y116"`.
  - Exclude "emergency contact, but not a guardian".
  - Normalize youth IDs so that "Youth 038", "youth 038", "Y038" and "y038" are the same child.

  - Exclude `daytime_first_aid` and past attendance ("only in the past", "used to attend").
- **c3 (train):** count only "genuinely prefers train to plane when both are available". Exclude "likes train and plane equally" and "chose train over plane (only) because of a disruption".
- **c4 (fruit):** count `enjoys eating` one of apple, banana, mango, orange, peach, pear, plum or watermelon. Exclude bread, rice, lentils and dairy foods. Exclude "dislikes eating banana" and similar.
- **c5 (dairy):** count "currently chooses to avoid dairy products", "avoids dairy products" and the first-person "I currently choose…". Exclude "temporarily skipping cheese/butter/milk/yogurt", "buys milk for other people" and "dislikes eating milk", since none of these is a current choice to avoid dairy.

## What could cause a wrong answer
- **Missing a qualifying person:**
  - a fact recorded under an alias, a lowercase name or a first-person "I" that isn't linked to the person
  - youth IDs written two ways ("Y038" and "Youth 038") and not unified
  - an unusual wording that falls outside the regex
  - a fact attached to the wrong name in a chained sentence
- **Including a wrong person:**
  - counting decoys: last-year events, emergency contacts, daytime or past courses, disruption-driven train choices, temporary skipping
  - counting the same event twice
  - crediting an ambiguous initial to the wrong person
  - treating "dislikes eating" or non-fruit foods as qualifying
