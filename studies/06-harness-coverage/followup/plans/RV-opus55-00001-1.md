The near-miss re-check is the weak point. It only looks at people the patterns already credited with three of the four conditions. A qualifying person whose patterns missed two or more conditions shows up as a "2 of 4" or worse and never gets re-read. The fix is to check coverage per sentence, not per person: every sentence that could be about an attribute either gets accounted for or gets read by a human (me). Then it doesn't matter how many of one person's facts were missed.

## Revised plan

Steps 1–5 stay as before. Step 6 is replaced with the following.

**6a. Wide keyword nets, one per condition.** These are separate from the strict patterns. The goal here is catching everything, not precision:
- **c1:** `work|employ|job|staff|hired|org(anization)?\s*0?\d+|based in|headquarter` plus every city name
- **c2:** `volunteer|helped (out )?at|event-|shift|pitched in`
- **c3:** `dairy|milk|cheese|lactose|butter|yogurt|vegan|plant-based`
- **c4:** `bus|train|transit|subway|metro|tram|ferry|commute|ride|pass`

I'd use Grep with `-i` and `output_mode: "count"` to size each net. Then a Python script in the scratchpad, run via Bash, tags every sentence with which nets and which strict patterns it hits.

**6b. Residual = caught by a wide net but not a strict pattern.** These sentences are where a missed fact would be. The script writes them to `scratchpad/residual_c{1..4}.txt`, each with its passage ID and any person it can resolve. I read all of them with Read and sort each one into: real fact (credit it to the right person), past/negated/planned (reject), or irrelevant. Because this goes sentence by sentence, a person with two or three facts in odd wording gets each one caught on its own.

**6c. Unaccounted-sentence audit.** This catches wording that none of my keywords anticipated. The script labels every sentence in the file as roster, org→city, strict attribute hit, wide-net hit, or none of these. I read every sentence in "none of these." Most should be filler. Anything that states a fact about a person is a phrasing I didn't foresee, and I'd add it to the patterns and rerun.

**6d. Unattributed facts.** Attribute sentences whose subject didn't resolve to an ID (pronouns, quoted speakers, unknown initials) go into their own file. I read each one alongside its whole passage to assign it or leave it out on purpose.

**6e. Recompute, then re-read every candidate's full file.** I rebuild the four-condition table with the corrected facts. For every match, and everyone now at 3 of 4, I grep all their names, aliases and ID and read every sentence about them. This step now guards against wrong inclusions rather than misses, since 6b–6d handle the misses.

**Fallback if the residual is huge:** if 6b plus 6c come to more than roughly 40% of the file, sorting it piecemeal isn't worth it. I'd read the whole file in order with Read, in chunks of about 1,500 lines, and record facts per person as I go. The script's output would then serve only as a cross-check.

## Rough added cost
- **Scripting:** one extra script pass, a few Bash calls. Small.
- **Reading:** this is the main cost. My guess is the wide-net residual is a few hundred sentences and the "none of these" bucket is a few hundred more. That's maybe 30–80 KB of text, about 10–25k tokens over 5–15 Read calls, against roughly 3–5k for the old near-miss check. I haven't measured it yet; the 6a counts would give the real number before I commit.
- **Worst-case fallback:** reading the whole 469 KB file is about 120k tokens and 25–35 Read calls, plus the time to sort it by hand.

In return, a miss now requires a fact sentence that avoids every keyword in every net *and* that I misread as filler in 6c. The old check let through any qualifying person with two or more missed conditions.
